**Who wrote it:** The claims platform team (a different internal team, not us) — code and data we cannot audit or diff, only add to our host's config.
**What it can reach:** Two claim-record lookups (`get_claim_status`, `get_adjuster_notes`); in this exercise it's an isolated stdio subprocess with no network/file access, but a real deployment would give it its own DB credentials or API token scoped to the claims store.
**What it logs:** Unknown to us — every claim number and every adjuster note we pull passes through their process, so anything logged there (claim numbers, note contents, our host's request patterns) sits outside our audit boundary.
**What a stolen token could do:** Read adjuster notes and claim status on every claim the token is scoped to — potentially the whole book, not just the claims our agent happens to query, and notes contain claimant names and incident narratives (PII).
**Ship or don't:** Ship for this exercise (stdio subprocess, no real credentials, sandboxed), but not to production without asking the platform team for token scope-down (status-only, no notes), their logging/retention policy, and an incident contact — none of which this exercise obtained.
